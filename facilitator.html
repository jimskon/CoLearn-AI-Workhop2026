The Day Before

Setup Checklist

Accounts & groups

  • On colearn-ai.com, the student (s1–s18) and creator (c1–c18) accounts are set up by server/scripts/makeWorkshopAccounts.js: s1–s13 under participants' names, s14–s18 spares. Write the password on the board on the day.
  • Print one login card per person: name, table number, student login (sN), and creator login (cN, the same number)
  • Two classes: CCSC:MW (code: ccscmw) for students in Part 2, and CCSC:MW Create (code: create) for creators in Part 3. In CCSC:MW, pre-create groups that match the table numbers on the cards, in both group activities (Loops with Turtles and How Maps Mislead), since each table chooses one on the day: set up the groups in one, then use Clone groups from activity in the other.
  • CCSC:MW holds six activities, in this order: Loops with Turtles (group), How Maps Mislead (group), Python While Loops (test), Feature Gallery (demo), Feature Gallery (group), and the Workshop Feedback survey (assignment). CCSC:MW Create starts empty.
  • Backup on csdev.kenyon.edu: the same accounts, password, classes, groups, and activities, so switching servers changes only the web address
  • Put three or four people per table, so every group has a Facilitator, a Process Analyst, and Quality Control
  • Tables are not assigned an activity: each table chooses programming or maps at the start of Part 2. Seating non-CS participants together makes the map activity an easy choice for them.
  • Five spare cards (s14–s18, c14–c18) for walk-ins: they write their name on the card. In View Groups a walk-in shows under the spare account's placeholder name; add them to a table group there.

Activities in the class

  • The programming activity, sized for 15 minutes (workshop_sampler_15min.txt)
  • The map activity (workshop_maps_15min.txt); its two Harbor County maps load from this site, so push before the workshop
  • Python While Loops (test mode) for groups that finish early. Check it opens for a student account: not hidden, no access code, and no start-time lock.
  • coLearn-AI Workshop Feedback (assignment mode) for the closing survey
  • Run each one end to end in the Sandbox the day before

Part 3 demo

  • A finished backup of the demo activity, in case the AI Builder is slow
  • One LLM revision prompt ready to paste
  • An LLM tab already open and logged in

Room

  • Projector showing a visible countdown timer
  • A tablet logged in as instructor, on View Groups in Observation view
  • Open colearn-ai.com on the room's guest Wi-Fi and on eduroam, to confirm both work
  • The arrival slide: private/workshop-logins.html (names, numbers, password, the two activities). On the Kenyon network it is also at cs.kenyon.edu/public/workshop-logins.html.
  • A ready-to-show backup slide: eduroam → csdev.kenyon.edu → same login
On the Day

Minute by Minute

TimeWhat happens · what to say · what can go wrong
8:50Arrival slide is up. Hand out cards. People sit at their card's table and log in as students. Risk: someone can't get online. Have them share a teammate's screen or use a phone hotspot.
9:00Part 1 talk (15 min). Cover bypass → Piaget and Vygotsky → 4 principles → turns and roles → accept/revise (say it works in any field, and point to the other-disciplines link) → Group A vs B. Skip everything else on the page. "In 15 minutes you'll be the students. Keep that in mind."
9:15Part 2 setup (7 min). Everyone opens the CCSC:MW class, and each table picks programming or maps (give them one minute; both activities already have the table groups). Roles are read aloud. "Facilitators: you're the clock. Call time at 9:42." Walk the room. At 9:20, every table should be on question 1.
9:22Activity (20 min). Keep View Groups (Observation view) on the projector so participants see the instructor's view while they work. Tag from the tablet. Tap a few tags, so the room has data for later. Go to tables that have been idle for over 2 minutes. At 9:37: "Five minutes. Finished? Try Python While Loops."
9:42Debrief (10 min). Call on three Process Analysts, one question each, one sentence each.
9:52Instructor view (8 min). Project View Groups. Show the timeline, the red and blue dots, and the submissions-per-question bars. Then open the trace for one question in two groups: same answer, different path. About 15 minutes after a table leaves its Part 2 activity, View Groups shows it as Ended – incomplete. That is expected, not an error; Reopen brings it back if needed.
10:00☕ Break. "Back at 10:10 sharp. Keep your card."
10:10Part 3 intro (5 min). The four guidance layers, and the one-question markup sample.
10:15Live demo (15 min). AI Builder → Visual Editor → one Copy for LLM round → Review Pasted Revision → Sandbox with one wrong answer. If the AI Builder takes more than 60 seconds, switch to the backup activity.
10:30Hands-on (45 min). Everyone switches to their creator login and opens CCSC:MW Create. Call each step out loud: plan at 10:33 · build at 10:40 · test at 10:50 · save at 11:10. Any table not in the Sandbox by 10:55 should cut down to one question. If the Visual Editor garbles an activity or shows an error, the fix is Undo. It is still under development.
11:10Save. "Copy your Source into an email to yourself now. These accounts are temporary." While they save, pick three tables to share.
11:15Share out (10 min). Three tables, 3 minutes each, one question each: what students see, the feedback prompt, what the AI did.
11:25Part 4 (15 min). The class-session checklist (6 min), then the lab-conversion example (6 min), then questions.
11:40Survey first (4 min). "Before we discuss, please take the survey. Log out of your creator account and back in with your student login, the s-number on your card. It's in the CCSC:MW class." Creator accounts can't take the survey; everyone has to switch back. Announce it at 11:38 so the switch fits in the 4 minutes.
11:44Research (5 min). The three research questions, with the Observation card as the example. Point people to the Research page.
11:49Discussion (11 min). Pick two prompts that fit the room.
12:00Close. Point to the Resources page: the demo, the papers, and contact details.
If colearn-ai.com is down or slow Switch everyone to csdev.kenyon.edu. That server is reachable only on eduroam. Show the backup slide: the logins and password are the same. Work done on colearn-ai.com before the switch does not carry over, so switch at a natural break if you can. People without eduroam share a screen with a teammate.
If you are running late Protect the 45-minute hands-on block and the survey. Cut in this order: the Part 4 lab example, the Part 3 share-out (down to one table), the Part 2 debrief (down to one Process Analyst).
