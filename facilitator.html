The Day Before

Setup Checklist

Accounts & groups

  • Reset passwords on the student (s1–s25) and creator (c1–c15) accounts
  • Print one login card per seat: table number, student login, creator login
  • In the CCSC:MW class, pre-create groups that match the table numbers on the cards
  • Put three or four people per table, so every group has a Facilitator, a Process Analyst, and Quality Control
  • A few spare cards for walk-ins and anyone off eduroam

Activities in the class

  • The programming activity, sized for 15 minutes (workshop_sampler_15min.txt)
  • The writing activity, sized for 15 minutes
  • The demo quiz (test mode) for groups that finish early
  • The closing survey activity
  • Run each one end to end in the Sandbox the day before

Part 3 demo

  • A finished backup of the demo activity, in case the AI Builder is slow
  • One LLM revision prompt ready to paste
  • An LLM tab already open and logged in

Room

  • Projector showing a visible countdown timer
  • A tablet logged in as instructor, on View Groups in Observation view
  • Check eduroam from the room itself
  • An arrival slide: join eduroam → csdev.kenyon.edu → log in with your card
On the Day

Minute by Minute

TimeWhat happens · what to say · what can go wrong
−0:10Arrival slide is up. Hand out cards. People sit at their card's table and log in as students. Risk: no eduroam. Give them a spare card, or have them share a teammate's screen.
0:00Part 1 talk (15 min). Cover bypass → Piaget and Vygotsky → 4 principles → turns and roles → accept/revise → Group A vs B. Skip everything else on the page. "In 15 minutes you'll be the students. Keep that in mind."
0:15Part 2 setup (7 min). Everyone opens the CCSC:MW class, and each table picks programming or writing. Roles are read aloud. "Facilitators: you're the clock. Call time at 0:42." Walk the room. At 0:20, every table should be on question 1.
0:22Activity (20 min). Watch the Observation view on the tablet. Tap a few tags, so the room has data for later. Go to tables that have been idle for over 2 minutes. At 0:37: "Five minutes. Finished? Try the quiz."
0:42Debrief (10 min). Call on three Process Analysts, one question each, one sentence each.
0:52Instructor view (8 min). Project View Groups. Show the timeline, the red and blue dots, and the submissions-per-question bars. Then open the trace for one question in two groups: same answer, different path.
1:00☕ Break. "Back at 1:10 sharp. Keep your card."
1:10Part 3 intro (5 min). The four guidance layers, and the one-question markup sample.
1:15Live demo (15 min). AI Builder → Visual Editor → one Copy for LLM round → Review Pasted Revision → Sandbox with one wrong answer. If the AI Builder takes more than 60 seconds, switch to the backup activity.
1:30Hands-on (45 min). Drivers switch to the creator login. Call each step out loud: plan at 1:33 · build at 1:40 · test at 1:50 · save at 2:10. Any table not in the Sandbox by 1:55 should cut down to one question.
2:10Save. "Copy your Source into an email to yourself now. These accounts are temporary." While they save, pick three tables to share.
2:15Share out (10 min). Three tables, 3 minutes each, one question each: what students see, the feedback prompt, what the AI did.
2:25Part 4 (15 min). The class-session checklist (6 min), then the lab-conversion example (6 min), then questions.
2:40Survey first (4 min). "Before we discuss, please take the survey. It's in the class."
2:44Research (5 min). The three research questions, with the Observation card as the example. Point people to the Research page.
2:49Discussion (11 min). Pick two prompts that fit the room.
3:00Close. Point to the Resources page: the demo, the papers, and contact details.
If you are running late Protect the 45-minute hands-on block and the survey. Cut in this order: the Part 4 lab example, the Part 3 share-out (down to one table), the Part 2 debrief (down to one Process Analyst).
