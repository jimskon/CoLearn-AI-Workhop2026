Activity 30 min

For Loops in Python — Activity Preview

The activity walks groups through Python for loops with range(), progressing from basic iteration to step arguments and accumulated computation. Each question follows the same structure: predict → respond → receive AI feedback → revise if needed.

Question sequence (representative)

  1. Q1 — Basic iteration: What does for i in range(5): print(i) print? Predict before running.
  2. Q2 — Start/stop: What does for i in range(1, 6): print(i) print? Does the stop value appear?
  3. Q3 — Conversion: Rewrite the literal-list loop below using range():
    for value in [3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18]: print(value, end=" ")
  4. Q4 — Step argument: Use range() to print every 4th integer from 4 to 40.
  5. Q5 — Accumulation: Write a loop that sums the integers 1 through n (user-supplied). What value does total hold after the loop for n = 10?

What to Notice As You Work

🔄 Rotation

The Spokesperson role passes after each submission. Notice how having a designated typist changes who speaks up during discussion.

💬 The AI Nudge

When the AI nudges your group, resist the urge to just ask it a follow-up. The nudge is a question — discuss it among yourselves first.

📜 The Trace

Watch the trace panel grow. See how your reasoning path — including predictions and revisions — is recorded separately from final answers.

👁️ Instructor View

The facilitator can see your trace in real time. We'll look at the instructor dashboard together after the activity.

Important rule Do not ask the AI to explain or solve. The AI in this activity is configured to nudge, not answer. If your group is truly stuck, talk to the Facilitator or raise your hand.
Debrief ~5 min at the end

After the Activity: Group Reflection

Before the break, each group's Process Analyst shares a one-sentence answer to one of these questions with the whole room:

  • Was there a moment where the group disagreed about the answer? What happened?
  • When the AI nudged you, did it help? Did it feel frustrating? Why?
  • How did having assigned roles change how your group worked?
  • What surprised you about the experience — from a student perspective?
Instructor view demo (facilitator-led, end of Part 2) After the group reflection, the facilitator will project the instructor dashboard and walk through what was captured: submission history, AI responses, revision counts, and the epistemic trace for two or three questions side-by-side across groups.
