Setup 7 min

Getting Started

  1. Connect to any Wi-Fi. The guest network is fine. Can’t get online? Share a screen with a teammate, or raise your hand.
  2. Open colearn-ai.com and log in with the student account on your login card. Each person uses their own account.
  3. Open the CCSC:MW class (see below). You are already in a group with the people at your table, and the platform has assigned each of you a role.
  4. As a table, start Loops with Turtles or How Maps Mislead (see below), and read your roles aloud.

Your card also has a creator login for Part 3.

Your Roles

The platform assigns roles in this order: Facilitator, Process Analyst, Quality Control, and then Spokesperson. A group of three has no Spokesperson.

●

Facilitator

Keep the group on task and watch the clock. Make sure everyone speaks before each submit. Call time at 0:42.

●

Process Analyst

Watch how the group works, not just the answers. You may be asked for a one-sentence reflection in the debrief.

●

Quality Control

Read the answer aloud before it is submitted. Is it complete? Does it actually answer the question?

●

Spokesperson (groups of 4)

Speak for the group. Ask the instructor questions and report to the room.

Who types? Whoever holds the turn. Only one person at a time can type. After each submission, the turn passes to the next member automatically, so everyone types at some point. When it isn't your turn, you contribute by talking.
Activity 20 min

Two Activities — Choose One as a Group

Your table decides together which one to do. Both run the same way: roles, turn-taking, AI feedback, and the epistemic trace. A good rule: pick the one closest to what most of your group teaches.

🐢 Programming Activity: Loops with Turtles

Loops with Python turtle graphics. Your group predicts what a turtle will draw, replaces repeated code with a loop, and makes a spiral. You see each answer as a drawing. Being comfortable with CS1 is plenty.

🗺️ Map Activity: How Maps Mislead

For colleagues outside computing, with no code and no background needed. Your group predicts how big Greenland really is, explains why the map fooled you, and compares two maps of the same data that tell different stories.

📝 Finished Early? Try the Quiz: Python While Loops

A short demo quiz shows the platform in test mode: individual, graded with a rubric, and no AI feedback. It’s optional. Take it if your group finishes before time is called.

The Submission Loop

💬

Discuss

Read the question together and talk it through. No one types yet.

⌨️

Respond

Whoever holds the turn types the agreed answer. Quality Control reads it aloud.

🤖

AI Evaluates

Accepted, or sent back with one question about what to rethink. No grades.

🔄

Advance or Revise

Accepted? Move on. Sent back? Discuss the AI’s question and try again. After a few tries you may press Continue.

The AI will not give you the answer. If it sends your answer back, resolve its question by talking it through as a group. Don't just rephrase the same answer.

What to Notice As You Work

🔄 The Rotating Turn

The typist changes after every submission. Who speaks up, and who stays quiet, compared with an unstructured group task?

💬 Being Sent Back

Did the AI’s question help, or was it frustrating? You’ll design these questions yourself in Part 3.

📜 The Epistemic Trace

Every submission, AI response, and revision is recorded. We’ll look at your group’s record right after the debrief.

Debrief 10 min · 0:42

After the Activity: Review Together

The facilitator calls on three Process Analysts. Each gives a one-sentence answer to one of these questions:

  • When the AI sent an answer back, did its question help? Or was it frustrating? Why?
  • How did the rotating turn change who contributed?
  • What surprised you, from a student’s point of view?
Instructor view, 8 min · 0:52 (facilitator-led) The facilitator projects the View Groups page for the groups in this room, in Observation view. It shows each group's timeline, which submissions were accepted and which were sent back, the number of submissions per question group, and the epistemic trace for one question across two groups. Notice how groups reached the same final answer by very different paths.
