Assign Your Roles Now

●

Facilitator

Keep the group on task. Call the Spokesperson rotation after each question. Watch the clock. Break ties.

●

Spokesperson

You type the group's agreed response. Wait for group consensus before submitting. Pass the keyboard after each question.

●

Quality Control

Read the group's answer aloud before the Spokesperson submits. Is it complete? Does it address the question?

●

Process Analyst

Observe how the group works together, not just the answers. You'll share a 1-sentence reflection at the end.

The Interface

What You'll See in the Platform

coLearn-AI opens one group of questions at a time; the next opens once the group submits. The group reads together, discusses, and the Spokesperson types the response. Understanding the interface before you start makes the activity run smoother.

The Submission Loop

Every question follows the same cycle regardless of question type:

💬

Discuss

Group reads the question, examines any code, and talks it through. No one types yet.

⌨️

Respond

Spokesperson types the agreed answer: text, code, or filled-in table cells. Quality Control reads it aloud.

🤖

AI Evaluates

The AI either accepts the answer or asks the group to revise, with a question pointing at what to rethink. No grades.

🔄

Advance or Revise

Accepted? Move on. Asked to revise? Discuss the AI's question and try again. After a few tries the group may choose to continue.

The Two AI Responses

✅ Accepted

The submission meets what the instructor asked for. The group moves on, and the success is recorded in the epistemic trace.

💬 Revise

Something is missing or not quite right. The AI asks one focused question about what to reconsider — it does not supply the answer. Discuss it together, then revise. After a few tries the group may choose to continue.

The AI will not give you the answer. Its job is to ask questions, not to teach. If the AI asks your group to revise, the goal is to resolve it through group discussion - not by rephrasing the same answer or asking the AI more questions.

Two Activities — Pick One

Activity 20 min

Both activities run the same way: roles, turn-taking, AI feedback and the epistemic trace. Pick the one closest to what you teach.

💻 Programming Activity

A short Python activity. Your group reads, predicts, writes and runs code together, and the AI responds to what you submit. CS1 comfort is plenty.

✍️ Writing Activity

For colleagues outside computing — or anyone curious how the platform works beyond code. Your group drafts and revises written responses with AI guidance that asks rather than tells.

📝 Finished Early? Try the Quiz

A short demo quiz shows the platform in test mode: individual, graded with a rubric, and no AI nudges. It’s optional — try it if your group finishes before time is called.

What to Notice As You Work

🔄 The Rotation

The Spokesperson changes as you go. Notice how this changes who speaks up and who stays quiet compared to a typical unstructured group task.

💬 When You’re Asked to Revise

Resist asking the AI a follow-up. Its question is for your group — discuss what it’s pointing at and resolve it yourselves.

📜 The Epistemic Trace

Every submission, AI response and revision is recorded. That record is what the instructor sees — we’ll look at yours together afterwards.

Debrief ~12 min

After the Activity: Review Together

Before the break, each group's Process Analyst shares a one-sentence answer to one of these questions with the whole room:

Questions for the Process Analyst

  • Were there a question where the group strongly disagreed on the answer? What happened?
  • When the AI asked you to revise, did its question help? Did it feel frustrating? Why?
  • How did having a designated Spokesperson change how your group worked?
  • Programming or writing: where did the AI’s questions push your thinking most?
  • What surprised you about the experience from a student perspective?
Instructor view demo - facilitator-led (end of Part 2) After the group reflections, the facilitator will project the instructor dashboard and walk through what was captured for two or three groups: submission history, AI responses, revision counts, and the epistemic trace for the same question across different groups. Notice how different groups reached the same final answer by very different reasoning paths.
