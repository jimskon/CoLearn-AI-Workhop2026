Research Collaboration & Discussion

⏱ 20 minutes 2:40 – 3:00 Open discussion

The final session is open — your questions, your ideas, your challenges. Below is context for the research directions and how to get involved. The facilitator will lead a discussion; this page is a reference you can return to after the conference.

Future Work

Closing the Loop — Where the Research Is Headed

The epistemic trace captures a rich signal. Three sources of that signal can be fed back to improve the guidance that shapes future interactions:

📄 Student Submissions & Revisions

Which questions caused the most nudges? Where did groups revise most? The trace makes this visible at scale.

👩‍🏫 Instructor Observations

Instructor notes during and after class can annotate the trace — "this group was clearly confused about X" — and become training signal for improving feedback prompts.

🔍 The Epistemic Trace Itself

LLM-powered analysis of the trace can surface where tighter or looser guidance is needed, and can suggest revised feedback prompts and sample responses.

Open Research Questions

  • Identify: Can agents reliably detect where tighter guidance is needed without requiring manual review of every trace?
  • Validate: Does improved guidance actually reduce cognitive bypass, or does it just increase AI push-back without changing student reasoning?
  • Surface: When should we share the trace directly with students as a reflection tool? What form should that take?
  • Measure: What metrics distinguish genuine engagement from strategic compliance — submitting revisions that the AI will accept without actually reasoning through them?
  • Scale: Does the activity format work differently for larger classes? For online sections? For students with varying levels of prior programming experience?
Design Tensions

Open Design Tensions — Your Perspective Matters

These are not solved problems. They are genuine design tensions where empirical evidence from diverse classrooms is needed:

Restrictive vs. Permissive

Tight guidance prevents bypass but can frustrate groups who are genuinely reasoning correctly but expressing it differently. Where is the right threshold?

Rotation vs. Efficiency

Forced Spokesperson rotation distributes responsibility but can slow the group. Should rotation be mandatory, optional, or adaptive to pace?

AI Feedback vs. Instructor Support

When should the instructor step in versus letting the AI handle it? What signals should trigger instructor attention?

Trace Privacy

The epistemic trace is powerful for instructors and researchers — but what should students have access to? Their own trace? Their group's? Anonymized class-wide data?

Get Involved

How to Participate in the Research

🧪 Pilot a Single Activity

The lowest-commitment entry point. Use one coLearn-AI activity in one class session. Share your feedback prompt, the trace, and your observations with the project team.

📚 Deploy for a Full Topic Unit

Use coLearn-AI for an entire unit (e.g., all Python loop activities in CS1). Provides richer trace data and a clearer picture of learning progression.

🔬 Co-design a Study

Formal or informal comparative study. If you have two sections, or can collect pre/post data, the project team can help design an IRB-friendly protocol.

✍️ Co-author Activities

Contribute activities to the shared activity library. Well-designed activities — especially for common CS1 and data structures topics — are the platform's most reusable asset.

Discussion

Discussion Prompts

The facilitator will open the floor. Here are some starting points:

For instructors who teach CS1 Python

  • What's the one concept students struggle with most — where you most want visibility into their reasoning process?
  • What existing lab activity would you most like to convert? What would the hardest part of converting it be?

For instructors who teach Data Structures or higher

  • Does the nudge model work for open-ended design questions, or is it better suited to closed, predictable-answer questions?
  • Can the epistemic trace capture algorithmic reasoning — e.g., tracing a sort or a tree traversal — in a useful way?

For researchers

  • What would a convincing empirical study of cognitive bypass reduction look like?
  • The trace is rich — what analyses would you most want to run on it?
  • How does this compare to other structured group-work systems you've seen or studied?
