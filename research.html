Research Statistics in coLearn-AI

Research questions Live classroom observation Metrics & exports Privacy

coLearn-AI records every group submission, every AI decision, and every revision as an append-only interaction trace. The research statistics features turn that trace into measures that address three research questions about AI-gated collaborative learning. These features also give instructors a live view of how each group is working during class.

The broader research program This work is part of ongoing research on collaborative learning, computing education, and AI-mediated learning environments.
↗ Research Site
Design principle: the trace is the source of truth. The pipeline runs in three stages. The raw trace comes first. Reconstruction is done by pure, unit-tested functions. Derived measures and exports come last. The platform records new data only where the trace cannot reconstruct a fact, such as who held the turn or when an instructor stepped in. Research instrumentation does not change how the platform behaves for students.
Research Questions

What We Are Asking

coLearn-AI makes two structural interventions. First, the group shares one activity, and turns rotate so a single student is responsible for each submission. Second, the AI gates progression: when reasoning is insufficient, it sends the answer back instead of letting the group move on. Each research question asks whether one of these mechanisms does what it is designed to do.

RQ1 · PARTICIPATION

Does enforced turn-taking produce equitable participation?

In unstructured group work, one confident student often does most of the work while the others watch. Rotating the active role is meant to spread both the work and the responsibility. Does it?

What is measured

  • Submit balance (0–1): normalized entropy of submits across members. 1 means perfectly even.
  • Turn balance: the same measure applied to turns held.
  • Dominant member share, and the share of runs where every member submitted.
  • Longest same-student submit streak.
  • Skipped turns, instructor-reassigned turns, and turn → first-submit latency.

How to read it

Balanced submits do not guarantee balanced thinking. A group can rotate the keyboard while one person dictates. That is why the live observation tags exist. Watch (one drives, others watch) and Talk (discussing together) give a human check on what the trace counts.

The Group Size breakdown asks whether equity holds for groups of 2, 3, and 4.

Discuss: What would convince you that participation was cognitively equitable, and not just equal in clicks?
RQ2 · REASONING & REVISION

When the AI blocks progression, how do student responses change?

When the AI sends an answer back, it explains what reasoning is missing. Do students rethink the answer, or do they make the smallest edit that might get through?

What is measured

  • Revised at least once / twice, and versions per question.
  • Normalized edit distance and character delta between attempts.
  • Code lines changed, and whether the program output changed.
  • Time from rejection to the next attempt.
  • Who revised: the same student or the next person in the rotation.

How to read it

These are measures of revision size, not quality. A one-word fix can be a real insight, and a full rewrite can be noise. For qualitative coding, the feedback_revision_pairs export places each answer before, the AI feedback, and the answer after side by side. This is where human analysis, or LLM-assisted coding, takes over.

Discuss: What coding scheme would you apply to before/feedback/after triples to tell genuine reasoning from strategic compliance?
RQ3 · PROGRESSION

Do students satisfy the AI's requirements through revision, or bypass them?

Gating is never absolute. After the allowed retries, a group can press Continue, and an instructor can force-advance a group. How often do groups earn their way forward?

What is measured

Every question gets exactly one outcome:

  • ai_accepted: the reasoning met the bar.
  • continued: the group used Continue after retries.
  • instructor_advanced: the instructor force-advanced the group.
  • unresolved: the group stopped there.

Supporting measures:

  • First-attempt acceptance.
  • Attempts to acceptance.
  • Eventual acceptance after an initial rejection.
  • Retry allowance used up.
  • Interventions after two or more consecutive rejections.

How to read it

A high Continue rate on one question usually says more about the question or its feedback prompt than about the students. The guidance may be too strict, or the expected answer too narrow. Breaking these outcomes down by question and by question type tells activity authors where to revise. This is the "closing the loop" idea from Part 5.

Discuss: Is some bypass healthy? Where should the line sit between productive struggle and a dead end?

This lets one instructor watch a whole room, see which groups are stuck, and record what they observe with one tap. Observation Tags

The tags record what the trace cannot see: how the group is behaving in the room. Each tag has a short behavioral cue so different instructors apply the tags consistently.

TagMeaningWhat to look for
TalkDiscussingTalking about the problem together.
CodeAll codingEveryone working on the code (code questions only).
WatchOne drives, others watchFocused on screens; one person types, the others watch.
QuietQuiet, on taskSilent but working: reading or thinking.
HelpAsking for helpHand up, or asking or waiting for the instructor.
FrustFrustratedSighs, head in hands, repeated failed runs.
OffOff topicSocializing, phones, other work.
?Can't tellNot visible, or unsure what they are doing.
Each tap captures its context automatically. Along with the time and the observer, each tag stores the question the group was on, whether it involved code, which student held the turn, how many seconds had passed since the last activity, whether an AI evaluation or code run was in progress, and the AI's most recent decision. A Frust tag can therefore be linked to the rejection that caused it, and a Watch tag to the student who was driving.
Analysis

Research Statistics Pages & Exports

A separate Research Statistics area aggregates across runs, activities, and courses. Shared filters are kept in the URL, so a filtered view can be bookmarked or shared. The filters cover courses, date range, group size, question type, and the idle threshold used for time on task (10 minutes by default).

Overview

Scale, headline results, data coverage, and every exclusion, with counts.

Participation · AI Gating · Revision

The core measures for RQ1, RQ3, and RQ2.

Time · Interventions

Active versus wall-clock time (pauses excluded, idle gaps capped), time between attempts, and instructor force-advances and rotations.

Group Size · Question Type · Over Time

The same measures broken down by group size, by question type (code writing, code reading, output prediction, debugging, explanation, and others), and by week or position in the course.

Honest numbers by construction. Every percentage shows its numerator, denominator, and N. Test runs, instructor sandbox runs, demo classes, and groups with no student members are excluded. These exclusions are counted and displayed, never silently dropped. Turn, intervention, and observation data are recorded only from the day the feature was deployed. Runs with partial turn data are set aside from turn metrics and reported as such.

Privacy

Privacy & Data Protection

The research features are designed so that researchers can study patterns without learning who the students are, and so that live monitoring records behavior, not content.

🔐 Pseudonymous IDs

Students, groups, and instructors appear in exports only as pseudonyms such as S-3f9a…. Each pseudonym is a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256) made with a secret that is held only on the server. A pseudonym stays the same across exports, so longitudinal analysis works, but it cannot be reversed without the server secret. If that secret is not configured, exports are refused.

🚫 Never Exported

Names and email addresses never leave the platform in research data. Student-level statistics include only accounts with the student role, so instructors and TAs who join a group do not distort the results.

👁 Access Control

Only instructors and activity creators can see research statistics, and only for their own courses. Administrators can see everything. The same rules apply to metrics, exports, run reconstruction, the live view, and observations.

📡 Signals, Not Content

The activity strip is built from on/off activity flags in 10-second slices, with no keystrokes or text. Local Sandbox work sends only a content-free "working locally" signal, at most every 10 seconds. Nothing typed in the sandbox is recorded.

One export contains student writing. feedback_revision_pairs includes answer text and AI feedback, because studying revision (RQ2) requires the words themselves. The interface labels it "Contains answer text." Treat it as identifiable data: students sometimes write their own names or personal details in free-text answers. Course names and activity titles are also exported as plain text. Research use of student data falls under each institution's IRB and consent process. The platform does not collect consent itself.
Status

Where the Work Stands

  • Built and in use: trace reconstruction, the participation, gating, revision, time, and intervention measures, the Research Statistics pages, the row-level exports, and the live Observation view with tags.
  • In progress: an analysis page and export for observation tags, so tags can be compared with all idle periods and not only the ones an instructor happened to notice. Also in progress is a published definition of every metric.
  • Optional tagging: authors can label questions with a question type. Every statistic works without these labels. Only the by-type breakdown needs them.
